**Q1. Tell me about your current role.**
"Full-stack developer at [Company], building and maintaining internal and customer-facing web applications for a healthcare tele-consultation client — Node.js/Express backends, React frontends, SQL Server/PostgreSQL/MongoDB/Redis, deployed on IIS and Cloudways. I own features end-to-end from requirements to production."

**Q2. Why do you want to leave?**
"I've delivered eight applications end-to-end and learned a lot by owning whole systems. I'm looking for a team environment with code reviews, larger-scale systems, and modern DevOps so I can keep growing."

**Q3. What are your strengths?**
"Ownership — I take a problem from a conversation with users to a deployed, supported system. Practical performance work — real optimisations like 92 s → 300 ms. And documentation — runbooks and specs that others can use."

**Q4. What is your weakness?**
"I sometimes over-invest in solving something myself instead of asking earlier. I've improved by time-boxing investigation and writing down what I tried before asking. Also, working solo meant fewer code reviews; I'm keen to work in a review culture."

**Q5. Salary expectation?**
"Based on my end-to-end production experience with Node/React and multiple databases, I'm looking for [X–Y] and I'm open to discussing based on the role." (Research the market range for your city and years of experience.)

**Q6. Notice period?** "[N] days; negotiable if needed."

**Q7. Are you open to relocation / remote / hybrid?** Decide beforehand and answer directly.

**Q8. Where do you see yourself in 3 years?**
"A senior full-stack/backend engineer who designs systems for a team — owning architecture, mentoring, and improving delivery with CI/CD and cloud."

**Q9. How do you handle deadlines?**
"Break work into the smallest shippable pieces, deliver the highest-impact part first (e.g., the report before the export), and communicate early if something slips — with a reason and a plan."

**Q10. How do you handle feedback / criticism?**
"As information. When seniors preferred log-only over email alerts, I implemented their choice but made logs actionable and documented the reason."

**Q11. Team vs solo?**
"I've proven I can deliver solo; I want a team because reviews and shared design make software better and make me better."

**Q12. What do you know about our company?** Research: product, tech stack, recent news; connect to your experience (e.g., "you use PostgreSQL and React — I've built reporting on 50M-row PostgreSQL tables").

**Q13. Why should we hire you?**
"Because I have done the full job already — requirements, design, code, deploy, support — on real production systems with real users, and I bring practical experience across SQL and NoSQL, queues, real-time, and deployment. I ramp up fast because I've reused the same disciplined structure across eight codebases."

**Q14. Any questions for us?**
- "What does the deployment pipeline look like today?"
- "How is the team structured, and how are code reviews done?"
- "What is the biggest technical challenge in the next six months?"
- "How do you measure success for this role in the first 90 days?"

**Q15. Gaps / short tenure questions?** Answer factually and briefly; focus on what you delivered.

**Q16. Can you work under pressure?** Use the "live CRM fix while two other projects were in progress" story.

**Q17. Availability for a technical assignment / live coding?** "Yes. I'm comfortable building a small Express + React feature live or walking through my repositories."
